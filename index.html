Tokyo guide for Karina

Pre-owned Louis Vuitton & a little rest

Hi Karina! You asked where to find pre-owned luxury bags in Tokyo. Since you are staying in Shinjuku, I start there. Then I add two more areas that are easy by train. I know you are mostly after Louis Vuitton, so I note which shops show it on their own sites. And because the moms may want a rest, there are a few good places to relax too.

Buying pre-owned Louis Vuitton

  • Date codes: Louis Vuitton bags carry a date code or serial stamp, and where it sits changes by year and by model. Ask the staff to show you where it is on the bag you like.
  • Ask for the shop's authenticity guarantee in writing, or ask how the shop checks its items.
  • Look closely at the corners, the handles and the zipper. These wear first, and shops grade condition from there.

01

Shinjuku

Your home base is also the easiest place to start. Several pre-owned shops sit within a few blocks of each other, and the rest options are close too.

Rest menu

Near Shinjuku. Pick one that fits your time.

  • 6

    Re.Ra.Ku Shinjuku

    Quick: 30 minutes

    A relaxation salon with 30-minute body care courses. Good if you only have a short break between shops. It is 1 minute from Shinjuku-sanchome Station (exit C3).

    Thermae-Yu Shinjuku

    Hot spring

    A hot-spring bathhouse in Kabukicho that is open 24 hours, with stone-bed saunas. Bathing is nude and separated by gender, so it suits those who like onsen. Plan 2 hours or more.

    Neighborhood bath near Koreatown

    A local sento (public bath) 5 minutes on foot from Shin-Okubo Station, the Korean town of Tokyo. Open 15:00-24:00 and closed on Saturdays. Pair it with a walk and snack in Shin-Okubo.

    1-15-17 Okubo, Shinjuku-ku

    Joule Spa & Wellness, Hyatt Regency Tokyo

    Hotel spa treat

    A hotel spa that is open to non-guests as a day spa, 11:00-20:00 daily. It has eight treatment rooms, including double rooms with city views. Book ahead.

    2-7-2 Nishi-Shinjuku, Shinjuku-ku

    Open in Google Maps

03

Ginza

Japan's classic shopping district. Three pre-owned shops are on or near the main streets, so it is easy to compare.

By train About 15 minutes from Shinjuku-sanchome on the Marunouchi Line to Ginza.

Plan a half day Half day: start at KOMEHYO Ginza at 11:00, walk up Chuo-dori to Daikokuya and Brand Off, then stop for tea.

Good to know

  • Tax-free shopping: bring your physical passport. Shops need to see it at the time of purchase.
  • Authenticity: each store has its own policy on guarantees and certificates. Ask before you pay.
  • Condition grades: shops grade each item. The scale differs by shop, so ask staff to explain it if you are unsure.
  • Many shops open around 11:00. Check the store's page on the day, as hours can change.
